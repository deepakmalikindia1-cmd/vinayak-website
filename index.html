Freight Guide

Frequently asked questions

New to importing or exporting? Here's a quick guide to how customs clearance and freight forwarding work.

Ask Our Team
What does a customs clearing & forwarding agent do?

A clearing and forwarding agent arranges the movement of your goods and gets them through customs. We book space with shipping lines, airlines and transporters, prepare documents such as the commercial invoice, packing list and bill of lading or airway bill, file customs declarations, arrange inland transport and coordinate delivery. You get one point of contact for everything.

What is the difference between FCL and LCL?

FCL (Full Container Load) means a whole container is booked only for your cargo. LCL (Less than Container Load) means your goods share a container with other shippers, and you pay only for the space you use. LCL suits smaller volumes; FCL is usually more economical and faster for larger shipments.

What affects freight charges?

The main factors are the mode of transport (sea, air or road), weight and volume, origin and destination, the routing, the type of cargo (for example hazardous or perishable goods) and the agreed Incoterms. Customs duties and taxes are separate from the forwarder's fee, so always ask for an itemised quote.

Which documents are needed for import or export?

Common documents include the commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading or airway bill and IEC (Importer Exporter Code). Depending on the product, you may also need a certificate of origin, licences or test reports. We'll tell you exactly what your shipment needs.

Do you handle small shipments?

Yes. Whether it's a few cartons by air, an LCL consignment or several full containers, every shipment gets the same care and attention.
